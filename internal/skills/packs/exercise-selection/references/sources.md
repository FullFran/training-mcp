# Sources: exercise-selection

Every entry was checked on 2026-11-25 against its live Crossref record (title,
authors, year, journal) and its PubMed record (PMID, abstract). Population,
design and limits below are taken from the abstract only, not the full text.
Keys are stable so they can later be linked to evidence ids.

### haugen-2023-machines

- Citation: Haugen ME, Vårvik FT, Larsen S, Haugen AS, van den Tillaar R, Bjørnsen T. Effect of free-weight vs. machine-based strength training on maximal strength, hypertrophy and jump performance: a systematic review and meta-analysis. BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil. 2023;15(1):103.
- DOI: 10.1186/s13102-023-00713-4
- PMID: 37582807
- Design and population: systematic review and meta-analysis; 13 studies, 1016 adults aged 18-60, at least 6 weeks; 5 studies measured hypertrophy.
- Finding: no difference in hypertrophy between modalities (SMD -0.055, CI -0.397 to 0.287); strength gains specific to the modality trained; choice down to preference and goals.
- Limits: few hypertrophy studies.

### kassiano-2022-variation

- Citation: Kassiano W, Nunes JP, Costa B, Ribeiro AS, Schoenfeld BJ, Cyrino ES. Does varying resistance exercises promote superior muscle hypertrophy and strength gains? A systematic review. J Strength Cond Res. 2022;36(6):1753-1762.
- DOI: 10.1519/JSC.0000000000004258
- PMID: 35438660
- Design and population: systematic review; 8 studies, 241 participants, all young men.
- Finding: some systematic variation seems to enhance regional hypertrophy and dynamic strength; excessive, random variation may compromise gains.
- Limits: no meta-analysis; young men only.

### fonseca-2014-variation

- Citation: Fonseca RM, Roschel H, Tricoli V, et al. Changes in exercises are more effective than in loading schemes to improve muscle strength. J Strength Cond Res. 2014;28(11):3085-3092.
- DOI: 10.1519/JSC.0000000000000539
- PMID: 24832974
- Design and population: randomised trial; 49 physically active individuals in four training groups and a control; 12 weeks, twice a week.
- Finding: whole-quadriceps growth similar across groups (about 9-12%); the varied-exercise groups grew all quadriceps heads, while the constant-exercise groups did not grow some heads significantly; constant intensity with varied exercises gave the largest squat strength gain.
- Limits: small groups; quadriceps only.

### maeo-2021-length

- Citation: Maeo S, Huang M, Wu Y, Sakurai H, Kusagawa Y, Sugiyama T, Kanehisa H, Isaka T. Greater hamstrings muscle hypertrophy but similar damage protection after training at long versus short muscle lengths. Med Sci Sports Exerc. 2021;53(4):825-837.
- DOI: 10.1249/MSS.0000000000002523
- PMID: 33009197
- Design and population: within-subject trial; 20 healthy adults; 12 weeks, seated leg curl with one leg and prone with the other.
- Finding: whole-hamstring volume grew more with seated (+14%) than prone (+9%) curls, in the biarticular heads but not the monoarticular one.
- Limits: one exercise pair; small sample.

### pedrosa-2022-rom

- Citation: Pedrosa GF, Lima FV, Schoenfeld BJ, et al. Partial range of motion training elicits favorable improvements in muscular adaptations when carried out at long muscle lengths. Eur J Sport Sci. 2022;22(8):1250-1260.
- DOI: 10.1080/17461391.2021.1927199
- PMID: 33977835
- Design and population: randomised trial; 45 untrained women; knee extension through full, initial (100-65 degrees of flexion), final (65-30 degrees) or alternating partial ranges.
- Finding: the initial (long-length) partial range gave greater relative hypertrophy in certain thigh regions than the other ranges.
- Limits: one exercise; untrained women only; regional measures.
