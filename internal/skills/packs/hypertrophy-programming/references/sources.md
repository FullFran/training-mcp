# Sources: hypertrophy-programming

Every entry was checked on 2026-11-25 against its live Crossref record (title,
authors, year, journal) and its PubMed record (PMID, abstract). Population,
design and limits below are taken from the abstract only, not the full text.
Keys are stable so they can later be linked to evidence ids.

### schoenfeld-2017-volume

- Citation: Schoenfeld BJ, Ogborn D, Krieger JW. Dose-response relationship between weekly resistance training volume and increases in muscle mass: a systematic review and meta-analysis. J Sports Sci. 2017;35(11):1073-1082.
- DOI: 10.1080/02640414.2016.1210197
- PMID: 27433992
- Design and population: systematic review and meta-regression; 34 treatment groups from 15 studies.
- Finding: each additional weekly set was associated with a larger effect size; a three-level comparison (<5, 5-9, 10+ sets per muscle) only showed a trend.
- Limits: few studies; weekly sets categorised coarsely; group averages.

### pelland-2026-dose-response

- Citation: Pelland JC, Remmert JF, Robinson ZP, Hinson SR, Zourdos MC. The resistance training dose response: meta-regressions exploring the effects of weekly volume and frequency on muscle hypertrophy and strength gains. Sports Med. 2026;56(2):481-505.
- DOI: 10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w
- PMID: 41343037
- Design and population: multilevel Bayesian meta-regressions; 67 studies, 2058 participants (79.1% male, mean age about 25).
- Finding: growth and strength rise with weekly volume with diminishing returns, more pronounced for strength; frequency's effect on growth compatible with negligible, on strength positive; counting indirect sets as half ("fractional") fitted best.
- Limits: mostly young men; the authors are coaches and writers in the fitness industry (declared).

### baz-valle-2022-volume

- Citation: Baz-Valle E, Balsalobre-Fernández C, Alix-Fages C, Santos-Concejero J. A systematic review of the effects of different resistance training volumes on muscle hypertrophy. J Hum Kinet. 2022;81:199-210.
- DOI: 10.2478/hukin-2022-0017
- PMID: 35291645
- Design and population: systematic review of randomised trials; 7 studies (6 in the meta-analysis) of trained adults aged 18-35, at least one year of training.
- Finding: no difference between moderate (12-20) and high (>20) weekly sets for quadriceps and biceps; high volume better for triceps.
- Limits: very few studies; young trained men; muscle-specific results.

### schoenfeld-2019-frequency

- Citation: Schoenfeld BJ, Grgic J, Krieger J. How many times per week should a muscle be trained to maximize muscle hypertrophy? A systematic review and meta-analysis of studies examining the effects of resistance training frequency. J Sports Sci. 2019;37(11):1286-1295.
- DOI: 10.1080/02640414.2018.1555906
- PMID: 30558493
- Design and population: systematic review and meta-analysis; 25 studies.
- Finding: no significant difference between frequencies when volume is equated, including in trained people and direct measures; non-equated studies modestly favoured higher frequency.
- Limits: group averages; frequency and volume are confounded in non-equated studies.

### schoenfeld-2021-continuum

- Citation: Schoenfeld BJ, Grgic J, Van Every DW, Plotkin DL. Loading recommendations for muscle strength, hypertrophy, and local endurance: a re-examination of the repetition continuum. Sports (Basel). 2021;9(2):32.
- DOI: 10.3390/sports9020032
- PMID: 33671664
- Design and population: narrative review proposing a paradigm; no new data.
- Finding: muscular adaptations can be obtained across a wide spectrum of loading zones.
- Limits: a review and position, weaker than a meta-analysis.

### refalo-2023-failure

- Citation: Refalo MC, Helms ER, Trexler ET, Hamilton DL, Fyfe JJ. Influence of resistance training proximity-to-failure on skeletal muscle hypertrophy: a systematic review with meta-analysis. Sports Med. 2023;53(3):649-665.
- DOI: 10.1007/s40279-022-01784-y
- PMID: 36334240
- Design and population: systematic review and meta-analysis; 15 studies of healthy adults of any age and training experience.
- Finding: trivial advantage for set failure over non-failure with any definition of failure (ES 0.19, CI 0.00 to 0.37); no advantage for momentary failure, nor for high versus moderate velocity loss.
- Limits: definitions of failure varied across studies; two authors earn income in the fitness industry (declared).

### robinson-2024-rir

- Citation: Robinson ZP, Pelland JC, Remmert JF, Refalo MC, Jukic I, Steele J, Zourdos MC. Exploring the dose-response relationship between estimated resistance training proximity to failure, strength gain, and muscle hypertrophy: a series of meta-regressions. Sports Med. 2024;54(9):2209-2231.
- DOI: 10.1007/s40279-024-02069-2
- PMID: 38970765
- Design and population: exploratory multilevel meta-regressions; reps in reserve estimated from intervention descriptions.
- Finding: growth increased as sets ended closer to failure; strength gains similar across a wide range of reserve.
- Limits: exploratory; reserve was estimated, not measured; the authors call for caution.

### plotkin-2022-progression

- Citation: Plotkin D, Coleman M, Van Every D, Maldonado J, Oberlin D, Israetel M, Feather J, Alto A, Vigotsky AD, Schoenfeld BJ. Progressive overload without progressing load? The effects of load or repetition progression on muscular adaptations. PeerJ. 2022;10:e14142.
- DOI: 10.7717/peerj.14142
- PMID: 36199287
- Design and population: randomised parallel trial; 43 adults (27 men, 16 women) with at least a year of lower-body training; 8 weeks, lower body only.
- Finding: adding reps at fixed load and adding load at fixed reps gave similar growth and strength.
- Limits: short; lower body only; two authors employed by a coaching company and one on an equipment advisory board (declared).

### hubal-2005-variability

- Citation: Hubal MJ, Gordish-Dressman H, Thompson PD, et al. Variability in muscle size and strength gain after unilateral resistance training. Med Sci Sports Exerc. 2005;37(6):964-972.
- PMID: 15947721
- Design and population: multicentre cohort at eight study centres; 585 adults (342 women, 243 men); 12 weeks of elbow-flexor training of the non-dominant arm.
- Finding: cross-sectional area changes ranged from -2% to +59% and one-repetition maximum gains from 0 to +250%.
- Limits: one muscle group and one programme; the abstract does not state training status.

### damas-2019-individual

- Citation: Damas F, Angleri V, Phillips SM, et al. Myofibrillar protein synthesis and muscle hypertrophy individualized responses to systematically changing resistance training variables in trained young men. J Appl Physiol. 2019;127(3):806-815.
- DOI: 10.1152/japplphysiol.00350.2019
- PMID: 31268828
- Design and population: within-subject trial; 20 resistance-trained young men; 8 weeks, one leg on a standard progressive programme and the other on varied variables.
- Finding: similar growth between legs; between-subject variability in growth about 40 times the between-leg variability.
- Limits: small sample; one muscle; young trained men only.
