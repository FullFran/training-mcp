# Sources: fatigue-autoregulation

Every entry was checked on 2026-11-25 against its live Crossref record (title,
authors, year, journal) and its PubMed record (PMID, abstract). Population,
design and limits below are taken from the abstract only, not the full text.
Keys are stable so they can later be linked to evidence ids.

### zourdos-2016-rir-rpe

- Citation: Zourdos MC, Klemp A, Dolan C, et al. Novel resistance training-specific rating of perceived exertion scale measuring repetitions in reserve. J Strength Cond Res. 2016;30(1):267-275.
- DOI: 10.1519/JSC.0000000000001049
- PMID: 26049792
- Design and population: cross-sectional; 29 adults (15 experienced squatters, 14 novices); single repetitions at 60, 75 and 90% of 1RM and one 8-repetition set at 70%.
- Finding: strong inverse relation between average bar velocity and the reps-in-reserve RPE (r = -0.88 experienced, -0.77 novices); experienced squatters rated the 1RM higher than novices.
- Limits: one exercise, one session; small sample.

### helms-2016-rir-application

- Citation: Helms ER, Cronin J, Storey A, Zourdos MC. Application of the repetitions in reserve-based rating of perceived exertion scale for resistance training. Strength Cond J. 2016;38(4):42-49.
- DOI: 10.1519/SSC.0000000000000218
- PMID: 27531969
- Design and population: practitioner article; no new data.
- Finding: describes how to use the reps-in-reserve RPE scale to adjust loads set by set.
- Limits: guidance, not evidence of effect.

### halperin-2022-rir-accuracy

- Citation: Halperin I, Malleron T, Har-Nir I, Androulakis-Korakakis P, Wolf M, Fisher J, Steele J. Accuracy in predicting repetitions to task failure in resistance exercise: a scoping review and exploratory meta-analysis. Sports Med. 2022;52(2):377-390.
- DOI: 10.1007/s40279-021-01559-x
- PMID: 34542869
- Design and population: scoping review and exploratory multilevel meta-analysis; 12 studies, 414 healthy participants.
- Finding: participants underpredicted reps to failure by 0.95 (95% CI 0.17-1.73) with very high heterogeneity (I2 97.9%); accuracy slightly better closer to failure, with 12 or fewer reps and in later sets; training status did not seem to matter.
- Limits: exploratory; studies explained and confirmed failure differently.

### greig-2020-autoregulation

- Citation: Greig L, Stephens Hemingway BH, Aspe RR, Cooper K, Comfort P, Swinton PA. Autoregulation in resistance training: addressing the inconsistencies. Sports Med. 2020;50(11):1873-1887.
- DOI: 10.1007/s40279-020-01330-8
- PMID: 32813181
- Design and population: narrative review with a conceptual framework.
- Finding: research uses terms such as adaptation, readiness, fatigue and response inconsistently; proposes a framework for operational definitions.
- Limits: conceptual; no pooled effect.

### pareja-blanco-2017-velocity-loss

- Citation: Pareja-Blanco F, Rodríguez-Rosell D, Sánchez-Medina L, et al. Effects of velocity loss during resistance training on athletic performance, strength gains and muscle adaptations. Scand J Med Sci Sports. 2017;27(7):724-735.
- DOI: 10.1111/sms.12678
- PMID: 27038416
- Design and population: randomised trial; 22 young men; 8 weeks of velocity-monitored squat training stopping each set at 20% or 40% velocity loss.
- Finding: similar squat strength gains; larger jump improvement with 20% (9.5% versus 3.5%) despite 40% fewer reps; more hypertrophy of vastus lateralis and intermedius with 40%.
- Limits: small sample; one exercise; needs a velocity device, which vibeTraining does not have.

### refalo-2023-failure

- Citation: Refalo MC, Helms ER, Trexler ET, Hamilton DL, Fyfe JJ. Influence of resistance training proximity-to-failure on skeletal muscle hypertrophy: a systematic review with meta-analysis. Sports Med. 2023;53(3):649-665.
- DOI: 10.1007/s40279-022-01784-y
- PMID: 36334240
- Design and population: systematic review and meta-analysis; 15 studies of healthy adults.
- Finding: trivial advantage for set failure over non-failure; no difference in growth between high and moderate velocity-loss thresholds.
- Limits: definitions of failure varied across studies; two authors earn income in the fitness industry (declared).

### singer-2024-rest

- Citation: Singer A, Wolf M, Generoso L, et al. Give it a rest: a systematic review with Bayesian meta-analysis on the effect of inter-set rest interval duration on muscle hypertrophy. Front Sports Act Living. 2024;6:1429789.
- DOI: 10.3389/fspor.2024.1429789
- PMID: 39205815
- Design and population: systematic review with Bayesian meta-analysis; 19 measurements from 9 randomised studies of healthy adults.
- Finding: small hypertrophic benefit for rests longer than 60 s, perhaps through more volume load; no appreciable difference beyond 90 s.
- Limits: few studies; substantial heterogeneity; one author formerly on an equipment advisory board (declared).

### grgic-2017-rest

- Citation: Grgic J, Lazinica B, Mikulic P, Krieger JW, Schoenfeld BJ. The effects of short versus long inter-set rest intervals in resistance training on measures of muscle hypertrophy: a systematic review. Eur J Sport Sci. 2017;17(8):983-993.
- DOI: 10.1080/17461391.2017.1340524
- PMID: 28641044
- Design and population: systematic review; 6 studies comparing rests of 60 s or less with longer.
- Finding: both short and long rests may be useful; a possible advantage of longer rests in trained participants.
- Limits: few studies with similar designs; no pooled estimate.

### bell-2023-deload-delphi

- Citation: Bell L, Strafford BW, Coleman M, Androulakis Korakakis P, Nolan D. Integrating deloading into strength and physique sports training programmes: an international Delphi consensus approach. Sports Med Open. 2023;9(1):87.
- DOI: 10.1186/s40798-023-00633-0
- PMID: 37730925
- Design and population: three-round Delphi of expert strength and physique coaches (34, 29 and 21 completing each round).
- Finding: consensus definition: "a period of reduced training stress designed to mitigate physiological and psychological fatigue, promote recovery, and enhance preparedness for subsequent training".
- Limits: expert opinion, not outcome data.

### coleman-2024-deload

- Citation: Coleman M, Burke R, Augustin F, et al. Gaining more from doing less? The effects of a one-week deload period during supervised resistance training on muscular adaptations. PeerJ. 2024;12:e16777.
- DOI: 10.7717/peerj.16777
- PMID: 38274324
- Design and population: randomised parallel trial; 39 resistance-trained young adults (29 men, 10 women); 9 weeks, with one group stopping training for the middle week.
- Finding: no appreciable difference in lower-body muscle size, endurance or power; the continuous group gained more lower-body isometric and dynamic strength.
- Limits: the deload was full cessation, not reduced training; upper body unsupervised; one author co-founded a coaching company and one sat on an equipment advisory board (declared).
